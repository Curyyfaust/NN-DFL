# III. COST-ORIENTED INTERVAL FORECASTING WITH VALUE-MARGINAL PROGRESSIVE LEARNING

## A. Cost-Oriented Interval Forecasting Problem

Let \(\mathbf{s}_t\) denote the information available at forecasting time \(t\), and let \(w_t\) be the subsequently realized wind power. A forecaster parameterized by \(\boldsymbol\theta\) produces

\[
(\underline w_t,\overline w_t)=f_{\boldsymbol\theta}(\mathbf{s}_t),
\qquad 0\leq\underline w_t\leq\overline w_t\leq\overline W. \tag{1}
\]

For a nominal \((1-\alpha)\) interval, conventional probabilistic forecasting minimizes

\[
\mathcal L_{\mathrm q}=\frac1N\sum_{t=1}^N
\left[\rho_{\alpha/2}(w_t-\underline w_t)+
\rho_{1-\alpha/2}(w_t-\overline w_t)\right], \tag{2}
\]

where \(\rho_\tau(e)=e[\tau-\mathbb I(e<0)]\). We use \(\alpha=0.1\). Although (2) gives a statistically meaningful interval, it treats equal-magnitude errors similarly irrespective of reserve availability, generator headroom, congestion, voltage margin, and imbalance direction.

We therefore define cost-oriented interval forecasting as

\[
\min_{\boldsymbol\theta}\quad
\mathbb E\left[\mathcal C(f_{\boldsymbol\theta}(\mathbf{s}_t),w_t)\right], \tag{3}
\]

where \(\mathcal C\) is the realized two-stage operating cost induced by the interval. A wide interval can increase preventive scheduling and reserve costs, whereas a narrow or misplaced interval can increase redispatch, curtailment, and load-shedding costs. The objective is consequently an economically allocated interval, not minimum width or maximum coverage in isolation.

### 1) Two-stage operating value

For \(\mathbf c_t=(\underline w_t,\overline w_t)\), the first-stage decision is

\[
\mathbf x_t^*(\mathbf c_t)=\arg\min_{\mathbf x\in\mathcal X(\mathbf c_t)}
C^{\mathrm{DA}}(\mathbf x)+\mathcal R(\mathbf x;\mathbf c_t), \tag{4}
\]

where \(\mathbf x\) contains active/reactive generation, upward/downward reserve, and grid exchange. After observing \(w_t\),

\[
\mathbf z_t^*(\mathbf c_t,w_t)=\arg\min_{\mathbf z\in\mathcal Z(\mathbf x_t^*,w_t)}
C^{\mathrm{RT}}(\mathbf z;\mathbf x_t^*,w_t), \tag{5}
\]

where \(\mathbf z\) contains redispatch, real-time generation and grid exchange, utilized/curtailed wind, and load shedding. Both stages satisfy explicit operating limits and network states generated by

\[
\boldsymbol\psi_t^s=\mathcal F_{\mathrm{PF}}(\mathbf p_t^s,\mathbf q_t^s),
\quad \boldsymbol\psi_t^s\in\mathcal S_{\mathrm{PF}},
\quad s\in\{\mathrm{DA},\mathrm{RT}\}. \tag{6}
\]

The realized interval value is

\[
\mathcal C(\mathbf c_t,w_t)=C^{\mathrm{DA}}(\mathbf x_t^*)+C^{\mathrm{RT}}(\mathbf z_t^*). \tag{7}
\]

Equations (3)--(7) define the forecasting problem rather than its solution method.

### 2) Difference from decision-focused learning

Decision-focused learning usually maps a point prediction into a single optimization decision and differentiates a decision loss through that optimizer. Here, two predicted boundaries jointly determine preventive day-ahead decisions and realization-dependent recourse. The learning target is the economic allocation of uncertainty, while the quantile objective remains active. The formulation is therefore cost-oriented probabilistic forecasting rather than point-prediction decision-focused learning.

## B. Physics-Coupled Two-Stage Cost Surrogate

Solving (4)--(6) in every forecasting update is expensive and does not provide a stable differentiable path to both boundaries. We construct a frozen value operator

\[
\widehat{\mathcal J}_{\boldsymbol\phi}(\mathbf c,w)\approx\mathcal C(\mathbf c,w), \tag{8}
\]

from solutions generated offline by the original optimizer. It is not an unconstrained interval-to-cost regressor. It preserves

\[
\mathbf c\rightarrow\widehat{\mathbf x}\rightarrow\widetilde{\mathbf z}
\rightarrow(\mathbf p^s,\mathbf q^s)\rightarrow
\widehat{\boldsymbol\psi}^{s}\rightarrow\widehat{\mathcal J}. \tag{9}
\]

### 1) Offline supervision

The training set is

\[
\mathcal D=\{(\mathbf c_n,\mathbf x_n^*,\boldsymbol\xi_n,\mathbf z_n^*,
\boldsymbol\psi_n^{\mathrm{DA}*},\boldsymbol\psi_n^{\mathrm{RT}*},C_n^*)\}_{n=1}^{N_s}. \tag{10}
\]

Retaining decisions and physical states in addition to costs forces the surrogate to reproduce the internal operating response instead of fitting only a scalar label.

### 2) Interval-conditioned day-ahead policy

The day-ahead mapping is

\[
\widehat{\mathbf x}=\Pi_{\mathrm{DA}}\left[g_{\boldsymbol\omega}
\left(\frac{\mathbf c-\boldsymbol\mu_c}{\boldsymbol\sigma_c}\right)
\odot\boldsymbol\sigma_x+\boldsymbol\mu_x\right]. \tag{11}
\]

The implementation uses a 2-64-64-14 SiLU network. The differentiable projection explicitly imposes generator, reactive-power, grid-exchange, and reserve limits, including

\[
0\leq\widehat r_g^u\leq\min\{\overline r_g^u,\overline p_g-\widehat p_g^{\mathrm{DA}}\}, \tag{12a}
\]

\[
0\leq\widehat r_g^d\leq\min\{\overline r_g^d,\widehat p_g^{\mathrm{DA}}-\underline p_g\}. \tag{12b}
\]

Thus, deterministic limits are imposed analytically rather than relearned.

### 3) Conditional real-time recourse

The interval, day-ahead decision, and realization are separately encoded:

\[
\mathbf h_c=E_c(\widetilde{\mathbf c}),\quad
\mathbf h_x=E_x(\widetilde{\mathbf x}),\quad
\mathbf h_\xi=E_\xi(\widetilde{\boldsymbol\xi}). \tag{13}
\]

Their output dimensions are 32, 48, and 24. The concatenated state enters

\[
\widehat{\mathbf z}^{\mathrm{std}}
=R_{\boldsymbol\phi}([\mathbf h_c;\mathbf h_x;\mathbf h_\xi]),
\quad R_{\boldsymbol\phi}:104\text{-}160\text{-}128\text{-}84. \tag{14}
\]

After inverse standardization, a differentiable repair layer gives

\[
\widetilde{\mathbf z}=\Pi_{\mathrm{RT}}(\widehat{\mathbf z};\widehat{\mathbf x},\boldsymbol\xi). \tag{15}
\]

It enforces

\[
0\leq\Delta\widetilde p_g^+\leq\widehat r_g^u,
\quad 0\leq\Delta\widetilde p_g^-\leq\widehat r_g^d, \tag{16}
\]

\[
\widetilde p_g^{\mathrm{RT}}=\widehat p_g^{\mathrm{DA}}
+\Delta\widetilde p_g^+-\Delta\widetilde p_g^-, \tag{17}
\]

\[
0\leq\widetilde w_r^{\mathrm{use}}\leq\xi_r,
\quad \widetilde w_r^{\mathrm{curt}}=\xi_r-\widetilde w_r^{\mathrm{use}}, \tag{18}
\]

together with grid-exchange and load-shedding bounds.

### 4) Nodal injection assembly

The repaired variables first form nodal injections. For example,

\[
p_i^{\mathrm{DA}}=-p_{d,i}+\sum_{g\in\mathcal G_i}\widehat p_g^{\mathrm{DA}}
+\sum_{r\in\mathcal W_i}w_r^{\mathrm{nom}}, \tag{19}
\]

\[
p_i^{\mathrm{RT}}=-p_{d,i}+\sum_{g\in\mathcal G_i}\widetilde p_g^{\mathrm{RT}}
+\sum_{r\in\mathcal W_i}\widetilde w_r^{\mathrm{use}}+\widetilde l_i^P, \tag{20}
\]

with analogous reactive-power equations and

\[
\mathbf w^{\mathrm{nom}}=\tfrac12(\underline{\mathbf w}+\overline{\mathbf w}). \tag{21}
\]

This retains the causal link between each adjustment and its network location.

### 5) Physics-unfolded power-flow operator

Both stages share

\[
\widehat{\boldsymbol\psi}^{s}=\widehat{\mathcal F}_{\boldsymbol\psi}
(\mathbf p^s,\mathbf q^s),\quad s\in\{\mathrm{DA},\mathrm{RT}\}, \tag{22}
\]

where \(\widehat{\boldsymbol\psi}^{s}=\{\widehat{\mathbf P}^{s},
\widehat{\mathbf Q}^{s},\widehat{\boldsymbol\ell}^{s},\widehat{\mathbf v}^{s}\}\).
Fixed subtree and path matrices encode the radial topology. The initial linear DistFlow state is

\[
\mathbf P^{(0)}=-\mathbf p\mathbf A_b^\top,
\quad \mathbf Q^{(0)}=-\mathbf q\mathbf A_b^\top, \tag{23}
\]

\[
\mathbf v^{(0)}=\mathbf1-2(\mathbf P^{(0)}\odot\mathbf r+
\mathbf Q^{(0)}\odot\mathbf x)\mathbf A_p^\top, \tag{24}
\]

\[
\boldsymbol\ell^{(0)}=\frac{(\mathbf P^{(0)})^2+(\mathbf Q^{(0)})^2}
{\mathbf v_{\mathrm{par}}^{(0)}}. \tag{25}
\]

Six nonlinear fixed-point steps are unfolded. At step \(k\),

\[
\bar\ell_{ij}^{(k)}=\frac{(P_{ij}^{(k)})^2+(Q_{ij}^{(k)})^2}{v_i^{(k)}}, \tag{26}
\]

and only this term receives a bounded learned correction,

\[
\widetilde\ell_{ij}^{(k)}=\bar\ell_{ij}^{(k)}\exp\!\left[
\gamma r_{\boldsymbol\psi}(P_{ij}^{(k)},Q_{ij}^{(k)},v_i^{(k)},r_{ij},x_{ij})\right]. \tag{27}
\]

Explicit DistFlow recursions update branch powers and voltages, and a learned damping factor combines iterates:

\[
\boldsymbol\psi^{(k+1)}=(1-\beta_k)\boldsymbol\psi^{(k)}+
\beta_k\widetilde{\boldsymbol\psi}^{(k+1)},\quad 0<\beta_k<1. \tag{28}
\]

Learning is therefore limited to damping and a small current-term correction; topology, line parameters, and branch-flow equations remain explicit.

### 6) Analytical cost and constraint layers

No neural cost head is used. The repaired decisions are evaluated by

\[
\widehat C=\widehat C^{\mathrm{DA}}+\widehat C^{\mathrm{RT}}, \tag{29}
\]

\[
\widehat C^{\mathrm{DA}}=\sum_g c_g^E\widehat p_g^{\mathrm{DA}}
+\sum_g c_g^u\widehat r_g^u+\sum_g c_g^d\widehat r_g^d
+c_0^{\mathrm{DA}}\widehat p_0^{\mathrm{DA}}, \tag{30}
\]

\[
\begin{split}
\widehat C^{\mathrm{RT}}={}&\sum_g c_g^u\Delta\widetilde p_g^+
+\sum_g c_g^d\Delta\widetilde p_g^-+c_0^{\mathrm{RT}}\widetilde p_0^{\mathrm{RT}}\\
&+c^{\mathrm{curt}}\sum_r\widetilde w_r^{\mathrm{curt}}
+c^{\mathrm{LS},P}\sum_i\widetilde l_i^P
+c^{\mathrm{LS},Q}\sum_i\widetilde l_i^Q.
\end{split} \tag{31}
\]

The power-flow states define voltage and line violations,

\[
\Phi_v^s=\frac1{|\mathcal N|}\sum_i
\left([\underline v_i^2-\widehat v_i^s]_+^2+
[\widehat v_i^s-\overline v_i^2]_+^2\right), \tag{32}
\]

\[
\Phi_f^s=\frac1{|\mathcal E|}\sum_{(i,j)}
[(\widehat P_{ij}^s)^2+(\widehat Q_{ij}^s)^2-\overline S_{ij}^{,2}]_+^2. \tag{33}
\]

The interval width induces the reserve violation

\[
\Phi_r=[\overline w-\underline w-\sum_g\widehat r_g^u]_+^2+
[\overline w-\underline w-\sum_g\widehat r_g^d]_+^2. \tag{34}
\]

Hence,

\[
\widehat{\mathcal J}_{\boldsymbol\phi}=\widehat C+
\lambda_{\mathrm{PF}}\sum_s(\Phi_v^s+\Phi_f^s)+\lambda_r\Phi_r. \tag{35}
\]

The value signal can consequently be attributed to energy scheduling, reserve, redispatch, curtailment/shedding, voltage, and line-capacity effects.

### 7) Identification and freezing

The power-flow operator is first trained against AC power-flow states and frozen. The recourse network is then identified through

\[
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{surr}}=\frac1{N_s}\sum_n
\left\|\frac{\widehat{\mathbf z}_n-\mathbf z_n^*}{\boldsymbol\sigma_z}\right\|_2^2
+\lambda_C\frac{(\widehat C_n-C_n^*)^2}{\sigma_C^2}. \tag{36}
\]

Because \(\widehat C_n\) is analytical, the second term promotes economic consistency without a free scalar-cost head. Validation reports cost and recourse errors, errors in \(P,Q,\ell,v\), and DistFlow physical residuals. All surrogate parameters are frozen before forecasting-model training.

## C. Value-Marginal Progressive Quantile Learning

### 1) Statistical initialization

Each LSTM, CNN, DLinear, or Transformer backbone is first trained with (2):

\[
\boldsymbol\theta^{(0)}=\arg\min_{\boldsymbol\theta}\mathcal L_{\mathrm q}. \tag{37}
\]

The upper boundary is parameterized as a nonnegative increment from the lower boundary, preventing quantile crossing by construction.

### 2) Boundary-value marginals

For realization \(w_t\), the frozen surrogate yields

\[
m_t^L=\frac{\partial\widehat{\mathcal J}}{\partial\underline w_t},
\qquad m_t^U=\frac{\partial\widehat{\mathcal J}}{\partial\overline w_t}. \tag{38}
\]

Their magnitudes measure economic sensitivity and their signs specify the beneficial direction of boundary movement. By the chain rule,

\[
\mathbf g_v=\nabla_{\boldsymbol\theta}\widehat{\mathcal J}
=m_t^L\nabla_{\boldsymbol\theta}\underline w_t+
m_t^U\nabla_{\boldsymbol\theta}\overline w_t. \tag{39}
\]

Unlike a heuristic error weight, (38) reflects the full two-stage and network-constrained response in (9).

### 3) Progressive transition

Direct value optimization from random initialization can produce unstable or degenerate intervals. The statistical contribution is reduced smoothly over epoch \(e\):

\[
\alpha_e=\alpha_{\min}+\frac{1-\alpha_{\min}}
{1+\exp[(e-e_0)/\kappa]},\qquad 0<\alpha_{\min}<1. \tag{40}
\]

Early updates preserve quantile semantics; later updates increasingly allocate uncertainty according to operating value. A nonzero \(\alpha_{\min}\) retains the statistical anchor.

### 4) Conflict projection and scale balancing

Let \(\mathbf g_q=\nabla_{\boldsymbol\theta}\mathcal L_{\mathrm q}\). When \(\mathbf g_q^\top\mathbf g_v<0\), only the conflicting value component is removed:

\[
\widetilde{\mathbf g}_v=\mathbf g_v-
\frac{\min(\mathbf g_q^\top\mathbf g_v,0)}
{\|\mathbf g_q\|_2^2+\varepsilon}\mathbf g_q. \tag{41}
\]

The directions are normalized,

\[
\mathbf u_q=\frac{\mathbf g_q}{\|\mathbf g_q\|_2+\varepsilon},
\qquad \mathbf u_v=\frac{\widetilde{\mathbf g}_v}
{\|\widetilde{\mathbf g}_v\|_2+\varepsilon}, \tag{42}
\]

and assigned the geometric-mean magnitude

\[
M=\sqrt{\|\mathbf g_q\|_2\|\widetilde{\mathbf g}_v\|_2}. \tag{43}
\]

The update is

\[
\mathbf g_e=M\frac{\alpha_e\mathbf u_q+(1-\alpha_e)\mathbf u_v}
{\|\alpha_e\mathbf u_q+(1-\alpha_e)\mathbf u_v\|_2+\varepsilon}, \tag{44}
\]

\[
\boldsymbol\theta_{e+1}=\boldsymbol\theta_e-\eta_e\mathbf g_e. \tag{45}
\]

The quantile direction remains the statistical anchor; only an economically motivated component that opposes it is projected.

## D. Training and Deployment

1. Solve the original two-stage model under sampled intervals and wind realizations to obtain decisions, physical states, and costs.
2. Train the topology-aware power-flow operator using AC power-flow data and freeze it.
3. Train the day-ahead map and conditional recourse model using optimization labels; validate the analytical cost and physical residuals, and freeze the complete surrogate.
4. Pretrain the forecasting backbone with the 0.05/0.95 pinball loss.
5. Starting from the pretrained parameters, calculate quantile and surrogate-value gradients for each batch.
6. Apply value-gradient conflict projection, scale normalization, and the progressive schedule in (40)--(45).
7. At inference, directly output \([\underline w_t,\overline w_t]\) from the forecaster.

No post-hoc calibrator, conformal expansion, or online optimization solver is used to generate the intervals. The nominal 90% level is defined by the 0.05/0.95 quantiles; empirical test coverage is reported without claiming a finite-sample guarantee. Whenever exact operating-cost evaluation is reported, it is obtained from the original two-stage optimizer rather than the training surrogate.

## E. Main Contribution

The framework establishes a structured connection between probabilistic forecasting and two-stage network operation. First, cost orientation is formulated as the economic allocation of lower and upper uncertainty boundaries rather than point-prediction decision matching. Second, boundary-specific marginal values are obtained through a surrogate retaining day-ahead/real-time coupling, analytical costs, and topology-aware power-flow states. Third, a progressive, conflict-controlled gradient update transfers these values to a quantile forecaster without discarding its statistical objective. These components form one unified cost-oriented interval-learning mechanism rather than a collection of post-processing rules.
